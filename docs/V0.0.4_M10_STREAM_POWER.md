# Orbit V0.0.4 — M10 Macro Stream-Power Erosion

Status: **implemented and verified**

Branch: `orbit`

Implemented: 2026-09-18

M10 is the large-scale terrain-shaping solver. It couples M09 drainage, M05
uplift, M04 authored intent and M02 geological competence without replacing
M11's later local hydraulic/material-transport simulation.

## 1. Governing model

Orbit uses the stream-power form

```text
E = K * Q^m * S^n * erodibility * erosionAllowance
```

where the selected drainage measure `Q` is either normalized contributing
area or normalized discharge, `S` is the M09 downstream slope, and the
erosion mask comes from M02/M04 physical inputs.

This is an incision/equilibrium solver, not a droplet erosion system.

## 2. Coupled equilibrium loop

Each M10 iteration performs:

```text
current M08 physical column
        ↓
rebuild M09 drainage
        ↓
M05 uplift/subsidence forcing
+ M04 equilibrium height correction
        ↓
stream-power incision
        ↓
M08 geological displacement
        ↓
M08 top-down erosion
        ↓
next drainage iteration
```

Drainage is therefore not frozen while terrain evolves. Valley incision can
change later routing and the next iteration consumes the changed physical
surface.

## 3. M05 uplift semantics

M05 `upliftMeters` remains a forcing amplitude, not a stamped final mountain
height.

`StreamPowerErosionConfig::upliftCouplingPerIteration` explicitly converts
that amplitude into geological bedrock displacement per equilibrium iteration.

Positive forcing raises the substrate. Negative forcing creates subsidence.

## 4. M04 authored constraints

M10 consumes:

- authored uplift already folded into M05;
- authored drainage guidance already folded into M09;
- authored height as a persistent equilibrium offset;
- protection as an erosion allowance.

Authored height is not added again every simulation step. M10 records the
initial physical surface and relaxes toward

```text
initial surface + authored height offset
```

so an authored +100 m landform remains a target rather than becoming +100 m
per iteration.

Protection scales incision only. It does not disable tectonic uplift or
subsidence.

## 5. Geological erosion mask

When bedrock is exposed, M10 resolves the M08 `RockTypeId` through the M02
geological material library.

Hydraulic erodibility is the primary incision coefficient. Hardness and
cohesion further suppress detachment.

Loose material uses the explicit M10 loose-material erodibility coefficient,
but any actual removal still passes through M08's canonical order:

```text
debris → sand → soil → regolith → bedrock
```

## 6. Stable channel floor

M10 never allows one incision step to cut a source below its receiver plus the
configured minimum bed slope.

The receiver prediction includes the same iteration's internal-page geological
displacement, avoiding a source/receiver race where both cells uplift
simultaneously.

For an edge-crossing flow, the receiver height comes from the M09 neighboring
physical halo.

## 7. Seam contract

M10 requires a complete M09 boundary halo.

It does not turn a physical-page edge into a solver boundary or ocean outlet.
Cross-page:

- conditioned drainage height;
- physical receiver height;
- incoming contributing area;
- incoming discharge;
- flow orientation

continue through the M09 exchange contract.

Cube-face rotations/reversals remain owned by the M01/world neighborhood layer.

## 8. Physical bake

The equilibrium solver runs on a copy of the M08 physical page.

The solved result retains two distinct quantities per cell:

- cumulative geological bedrock displacement;
- cumulative stream-power incision.

`ApplyStreamPowerErosionResult()` bakes them in that order:

1. move bedrock and its mass-accounting reference;
2. erode the M08 material stack.

This distinction prevents tectonic subsidence from being counted as excavated
rock and preserves loose-before-bedrock removal.

## 9. M08 extension

M10 adds `MaterialColumnPage::DisplaceBedrock()`.

The operation moves:

- current bedrock elevation;
- optionally the reference bedrock elevation;

while leaving loose layer depths unchanged.

This is the physical operation used by uplift/subsidence and other later
geological displacement processes.

## 10. Determinism and revision identity

`StreamPowerRevisionFingerprint()` includes:

- complete physical page identity;
- M02 library revision;
- M09 halo revision;
- M10 configuration;
- all sampled M05/M04 forcing values.

Camera position, GPU slot, clipmap phase and cache residency are absent.

The CPU equilibrium path is deterministic for identical inputs.

## 11. Acceptance coverage

`Orbit.TerrainStreamPower` verifies:

- drainage area strengthens downstream incision;
- the final M10 solve can be baked back to the exact M08 surface;
- competent basalt incises slower than soft volcanic material;
- M04 full protection suppresses incision;
- M05 uplift raises terrain without appearing as bedrock excavation;
- authored height behaves as an equilibrium target instead of a repeated stamp;
- drainage area continues across a physical page seam;
- stream-power slope remains continuous across that seam;
- identical inputs produce identical revision and terrain results.

M08 coverage additionally verifies uplift/subsidence displacement preserves
loose material and does not corrupt excavation mass accounting.

## 12. Reference direction

The implementation follows the large-scale stream-power equilibrium direction
from Schott et al. 2023: an uplift field competes with fluvial incision to
produce authored mountainous drainage structure.

Orbit differs where required by the engine architecture:

- routing comes from M09's seam-safe physical-page drainage;
- geology comes from M02 rather than a generic noise multiplier;
- authored protection/height comes from M04;
- physical material removal uses M08;
- page boundaries are exchanged explicitly instead of fixed to zero.
