M15 adds climate-gated glacier evolution, local ice flow, glacial erosion,
U-shaped valley widening, debris transport and moraine deposition while
preserving the M08 physical-terrain and M14 shared-sediment authorities.

## 1. Climate / ice eligibility

Each physical cell receives a coarse climate/process forcing:

```text
mean annual temperature
snowfall in metres ice-equivalent / year
initial ice thickness
process mask
terrain protection
```

The frozen eligibility multiplier is:

```text
eligibility
  = cold-temperature factor
  × snowfall factor
  × authored/process mask
```

It is clamped to `[0,1]`.

A zero process mask or zero evaluated eligibility is a hard M15 boundary:

- no retained glacier ice;
- no accumulation;
- no glacier flow;
- no terrain erosion;
- no lateral widening;
- no moraine process.

Acceptance explicitly verifies that masked cells retain their original M08
terrain state.

## 2. Ice state and surface mass balance

M15 stores per-cell glacier thickness as process state.

One climate step applies:

```text
ice
+ snowfall × accumulation efficiency
- temperature-driven ablation
```

Ablation is bounded by the ice actually available.

The result reports:

- initial ice volume;
- accumulated volume;
- ablated volume;
- final volume;
- absolute and relative volume-balance error.

Internal glacier transport is conservative and does not appear as a source or
sink in the ice balance.

## 3. Glacier-flow approximation

M15 uses an efficient local shallow-ice-inspired approximation rather than a
full Stokes solver.

For every glaciated cell:

1. evaluate the current ice-surface elevation:
   `M08 physical surface + ice thickness`;
2. find the steepest lower eligible D8 neighbour;
3. compute local ice-surface slope;
4. evaluate speed from normalized ice thickness and surface slope;
5. clamp speed and the per-step transported fraction;
6. move the corresponding ice volume using a Jacobi-style volume update.

The speed model is configurable through:

- reference ice thickness;
- reference surface slope;
- reference speed;
- thickness exponent;
- slope exponent;
- maximum speed;
- maximum per-step flow fraction.

This gives deterministic downhill glacier spreading while keeping the process
cheap enough for large terrain-process epochs.

## 4. Glacial erosion

Terrain erosion remains an M08 physical mutation.

The local erosion rate scales with:

- glacier speed;
- ice thickness / overburden proxy;
- climate eligibility;
- authored protection;
- intrinsic M02 rock response.

For exposed bedrock, M15 derives a glacial susceptibility from existing M02
intrinsic properties:

- hardness;
- cohesion;
- fracture tendency.

Competent rock therefore evolves more slowly, while weak/fractured substrate
abrades and plucks more readily.

Loose M08 material is eroded directly through the normal top-down material
stack.

## 5. U-shaped valley widening

M15 does not obtain glacial morphology by blurring the heightfield.

After resolving the actual local glacier-flow direction, the solver derives the
two perpendicular side directions.

The basal erosion depth drives a separate configurable lateral-erosion term on
those two valley-wall cells.

Therefore glacier geometry explicitly contains:

```text
basal incision
+
flow-perpendicular wall erosion
```

The acceptance suite runs the same V-shaped starting valley through M15 and the
M11 hydraulic reference. It requires the glacial result to produce a
measurably larger shoulder/centre widening ratio, demonstrating U-shaped
widening rather than merely deeper incision.

## 6. M14 glacial sediment classification

M14 now recognizes `SedimentSourceProcess::GlacialErosion`.

Existing loose M08 material keeps the canonical M14 classification.

Freshly eroded bedrock uses a mixed glacial load:

```text
25% sand
25% fines
50% coarse debris
```

by default.

The fractions are explicit `SedimentConversionRules` values and remain
configurable.

The mixed load models abrasion flour/sand plus plucked coarse till without
creating a glacier-only material type.

## 7. Debris entrainment

Every M15 physical removal is published through the shared M14
`surfaceMobile` medium.

Basal debris remains at the glacier cell.

Material eroded from either lateral wall is moved into the source glacier
cell's M14 surface-mobile load, representing entrainment into/on the ice rather
than leaving lateral talus in a process-private buffer.

There is no M15-specific persistent sediment authority.

## 8. Debris transport

The same local ice-flow fraction is used to move a configurable fraction of
M14 surface-mobile sediment toward the glacier-flow receiver.

All three M14 classes remain available:

- sand;
- fines;
- coarse debris.

Transport is performed through M14 `Take()` / `Add()` operations, preserving
typed mass exactly.

## 9. Moraine deposition

Thin or stagnant ice progressively deposits its M14 surface-mobile load back
into the physical M08 column.

The settling strength is the larger of:

- thin-ice terminus factor;
- low-speed/stagnation factor.

The resulting deposition fraction is controlled by the moraine deposition
rate and timestep.

Actual material placement uses the canonical M14 → M08 deposition path, so the
existing deposition priority remains:

```text
coarse debris
sand
fines
```

Moraine material is therefore ordinary physical M08 debris/sand/soil and is
immediately available to later terrain processes.

## 10. Material conservation

M15 reports an independent physical/material balance:

```text
initial loose mass
+ newly excavated bedrock mass
=
final loose mass
+ final M14 mobile sediment
```

Moraine deposition is an internal M14 → M08 transfer and therefore does not
change the global balance.

The acceptance suite requires the relative material error to remain below
`3×10^-5`.

## 11. Process diagnostics

Each result cell exposes:

- climate eligibility;
- ice thickness;
- ice-surface elevation;
- D8 flow direction;
- flow speed;
- outgoing ice volume;
- cumulative basal erosion;
- cumulative lateral erosion;
- cumulative eroded mass;
- cumulative transported sediment;
- cumulative moraine deposition.

These fields are process/debug state and do not become terrain authority.

## 12. Determinism and residency

The M15 CPU process contains no stochastic ordering.

Fixed:

- M08 material state;
- M02 geological library;
- climate field;
- M14 conversion rules;
- M15 configuration;

produce identical:

- ice state;
- flow routing;
- erosion;
- sediment transport;
- moraine deposition;
- final physical terrain.

Camera/cache residency is not part of the process identity.

## 13. Acceptance coverage

`Orbit.TerrainGlacialErosion` verifies:

- climate/process mask is a hard no-glacier/no-erosion boundary;
- internal ice flow conserves volume;
- ice moves down the ice-surface gradient;
- glacier forcing widens a V-shaped valley measurably more than the hydraulic
  reference, producing the intended U-shaped signature;
- glacial erosion publishes coarse debris and fines through M14;
- thin/stagnant ice deposits moraine through M14 → M08;
- physical moraine debris exists in M08;
- M08 + M14 material mass remains conserved;
- fixed inputs are deterministic.

The existing `Orbit.TerrainSedimentExchange` suite was also extended to verify
that M15 glacial classification preserves removal mass and produces a mixed
till/abrasion load.

## 15. Reference direction

The architecture follows the frozen M15 direction from Cordonnier et al.,
*Forming Terrains by Glacial Erosion* (ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH 2023): couple glacier
flow and terrain evolution across different timescales, include debris
transport, and produce morphology specific to glaciers rather than reusing
fluvial erosion.

Orbit uses its own M08/M14 authority and local page architecture rather than
copying a reference implementation.
