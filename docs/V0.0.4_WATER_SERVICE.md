# Orbit V0.0.4 — WaterService Scaffold

Status: **complete — normative V0.0.4 WaterService acceptance closed on 2026-09-20**

Branch target: `orbit`

This document is part of the V0.0.4 implementation scaffold and must be treated as a dependency of the terrain/erosion/biome milestones in `docs/V0.0.4_SPEC.md`.

The permanent authority chain is:

```text
Geology
  ↓
Physical Material Column
  ↓
Drainage / Macro Geomorphology
  ↓
WaterService
  ├─ optional ocean reservoir
  ├─ sources / sinks / barriers
  ├─ dynamic water pages
  ├─ hull exclusion / flooding domains
  └─ force / momentum interaction
  ↓
Terrain Processes
  ↓
Exposed Surface State
  ↓
Biome Dressing
  ↓
Material + Scatter Resolution
  ↓
Persistent GPU Terrain + Water Caches
  ↓
Rendering
```

The defining WaterService invariants are:

> **The ocean is a terminal hydrological reservoir, not a deletion sink.**

> **A sealed mesh may exclude exterior water rendering without deleting physical water.**

> **World objects may inject conservative momentum, angular momentum, volume, pressure and vorticity into active water.**

> **Render-only waves cannot cause authoritative flooding; gameplay-significant waves must exist in physical water state.**

## 1. Authority split

`WaterService` owns:

- water mass;
- free-surface state;
- momentum;
- optional planetary ocean reservoir and datum;
- water sources and sinks;
- barriers, dams, gates and spillways;
- dynamic shallow-water pages;
- sleeping/waking policy;
- water-domain connectivity;
- hull exclusion masks;
- interior flooding domains;
- wave/disturbance emitters;
- momentum/volume/pressure/vorticity injection;
- fluid physical definitions;
- data exposed to water rendering.

`TerrainProcessService` owns:

- hydraulic erosion;
- sediment entrainment and deposition;
- terrain response to water forcing.

The biome system may inspect water/moisture state but does not own water.

The renderer may visualize water but never owns hydrological state.

## 2. Core fluid data model

### Ocean definition

```cpp
struct OceanDefinition
{
    bool enabled;
    double datumHeightMeters;
    FluidId fluid;
};
```

The ocean is a connected reservoir around a reference datum. It may be disabled entirely.

Hydrologically:

```text
ocean = terminal connected reservoir
```

Physically:

```text
ocean = dynamic water body
```

Water reaching the sea is not deleted at the coast. It enters the connected reservoir and can transfer momentum into active ocean pages.

### Dynamic water cell

```cpp
struct WaterCell
{
    float depth;
    float momentumX;
    float momentumY;
    float suspendedSediment;
};
```

### Fluid asset

```cpp
struct FluidDefinition
{
    float density;
    float dynamicViscosity;
    float surfaceTension;

    FluidSurfaceMaterialId surfaceMaterial;
};
```

Physics and rendering are separate. A custom fluid shader may alter appearance but cannot alter density, volume conservation, or solver semantics.

The standard shallow-water solver is valid for water-like free-surface fluids. High-viscosity/non-Newtonian fluids require a solver explicitly validated for those regimes; setting a viscosity value must not silently pretend the ordinary water solver remains physically valid.

### Authored water entities

```cpp
struct WaterSource;
struct WaterSink;
struct WaterBarrier;
struct WaterGate;
struct WaterSpillway;
struct WaterDomain;
struct WaveEmitter;
```

Sources should support point, spline, and area placement.

## 3. Ocean reservoir and wave semantics

The ocean is the final hydrological sink but remains physically interactive.

```text
river / runoff / authored source
        ↓
dynamic inland water
        ↓
coast transition
        ↓
ocean reservoir
```

Authoritative ocean surface:

```text
surfaceHeight =
    oceanDatum
  + longWaveHeight
  + localDynamicHeight
```

High-frequency spectral/chop displacement may be added by rendering:

```text
renderedSurface = authoritativeSurface + visualWaveDisplacement
```

but visual displacement cannot modify wet/dry state or authoritative flood volume.

A user may author real waves through a disturbance object:

```text
WaveEmitter
  ↓
physical elevation / momentum disturbance
  ↓
dynamic water page wakes
  ↓
wave propagation
  ↓
bathymetry interaction
  ↓
shoreline run-up / flooding if energetic enough
```

The same path supports tsunami-like events, landslides, explosions, storm surge, vessel displacement, and other gameplay-relevant disturbances.

## 4. Dynamic shallow-water pages

Orbit must not simulate an entire planetary ocean at gameplay resolution.

Use a tiered representation:

```text
Global reservoir
  datum / connected volume / coarse state

Far or inactive open ocean
  analytic/static/spectral representation

Sleeping dynamic page
  preserved state, no active stepping

Active dynamic page
  conservative shallow-water solve

Future high-detail local domain
  only for interactions that cannot be represented by shallow water
```

Dynamic pages store depth and momentum and use robust wet/dry handling.

Required boundary types:

```cpp
enum class WaterBoundaryType
{
    Closed,
    Open,
    Reservoir,
    Source,
    Drain
};
```

The ocean uses `Reservoir`, not `Drain`.

Camera movement does not wake pages. Physical forcing or connectivity changes wake them.

Pages require halo/ghost cells and explicit boundary flux exchange so flood waves, rivers, wakes, and sediment transport can cross page boundaries without seams.

## 5. Water geometry masking

Water rendering and physical water are separate concepts.

A sealed vessel must not render the exterior water surface through its interior.

```cpp
struct WaterExclusionVolume
{
    EntityId owner;
    HullMaskId hullMask;
    bool sealedInterior;
};
```

Supported masking representations should include:

- simple convex/primitive volumes;
- watertight mesh-derived mask;
- signed-distance field or compact voxel occupancy for complex hulls.

Preferred complex-hull representation is an SDF/occupancy field because it can be reused for:

- render masking;
- submergence queries;
- buoyancy;
- displacement volume;
- interior/exterior classification;
- future flooding/compartment connectivity.

Invariant:

```text
render mask != physical drain
```

Enabling/disabling a hull water mask cannot change water mass.

---

## 6. Interior flooding domains

Do not disable an entire vessel mask when a breach occurs.

A breach is a connection between domains:

```text
exterior water
      ↓
opening / breach
      ↓
InteriorWaterDomain
```

The V0.0.4 architecture must be compartment-ready even if full ship compartment simulation is implemented later.

Future systems must be possible without replacing the API:

- watertight bulkheads;
- doors/hatches;
- progressive flooding;
- bilge pumps;
- free-surface stability effects;
- separate flooded/sealed compartments.

Exterior/interior exchange must conserve mass.

## 7. Generic force and disturbance interaction

Do not create a one-off `PropellerWashSystem`.

Expose generic water forcing:

```cpp
WaterService::InjectMomentum(...);
WaterService::InjectAngularMomentum(...);
WaterService::InjectVolume(...);
WaterService::InjectPressure(...);
WaterService::InjectVorticity(...);
```

Generic emitter:

```cpp
struct FluidForceEmitter
{
    vec3 position;
    vec3 direction;

    float radius;
    float linearImpulse;
    float angularImpulse;
    float volumeRate;
    float pressureImpulse;
    float turbulence;

    FluidId targetFluid;
};
```

Users include:

- propellers;
- waterjets;
- pumps;
- waterfalls;
- spillways;
- explosions;
- falling objects;
- hull displacement;
- authored wave machines;
- future rotor/downwash interaction with water.

---

## 8. Propeller wash

Normal gameplay must not require blade-resolved CFD.

Use an actuator-disk/propeller-disk style source as the baseline physical approximation.

```text
propeller model
    ↓
computes thrust / torque
    ↓
boat receives +force / +moment
water receives reaction momentum / swirl
    ↓
prop wash
    ↓
wake / turbulence / surface disturbance
```

The source should support:

- axial momentum;
- radial distribution;
- angular/swirl momentum;
- optional turbulence scalar;
- optional cavitation indicator for rendering;
- reverse thrust;
- rotation-direction-dependent swirl.

Momentum coupling must be conservative within numerical tolerance.

Foam/cavitation are render consequences of physical fields, not the source of flow.

# 14. V0.0.4 WaterService completion gate

WaterService work is complete for V0.0.4 only when:

- ocean can be independently enabled/disabled;
- ocean datum controls the base planetary sea level/coastline;
- ocean behaves as a connected terminal reservoir rather than deleting water;
- authored/user disturbances can create physical waves on ocean water;
- gameplay-significant waves can propagate and flood low shore terrain;
- water sources can produce streams/rivers that ultimately connect to reservoirs/ocean;
- dams/barriers/gates/spillways can alter runtime flow;
- dynamic water pages sleep and wake from physical need rather than camera movement;
- watertight hulls do not visually contain the exterior ocean surface;
- render masking cannot alter physical mass;
- breaches can connect exterior water to interior flooding domains;
- moving hulls can displace water;
- propellers can transfer reaction momentum/swirl into water and create physical prop wash;
- custom fluid rendering is decoupled from fluid physics;
- water state couples into hydraulic erosion and sediment transport without giving WaterService authority over terrain geology.

# 15. Completion record

The production-shaped `terrain_water::WaterService` is owned automatically by every rocky body's `TerrainBodyServices`. It freezes the CPU authority and derived-runtime boundaries for fluid assets, optional ocean datum/reservoir semantics, sparse dynamic pages, conservative internal and cross-page flux, source/barrier/gate control, physical versus visual waves, physical wake/sleep, hull render masks, interior flood-domain exchange, hull displacement, and generic momentum/angular-momentum/volume/pressure/vorticity emitters.

`WorldSchemas` supplies stable command/document identities for Water Service, source, barrier/gate/spillway, interior-domain and force/wave-emitter objects. The schema-driven Studio inspector therefore exposes these authored records through the existing transaction/undo/persistence path. `SampleForTerrain()` is the read-only hydraulic/sediment coupling boundary; render materials remain separate from `FluidDefinition` physics.

`Orbit.WaterService` covers all 14 mandatory deterministic behavior groups, including dam-break conservation, wet/dry propagation, page-edge wake/flux, mask/mass separation, breach conservation, displacement, propeller reaction/swirl and renderer/physics separation. The warnings-as-errors V0.0.4 workflow and `run_m30_performance.bat` both build and execute this gate before M31 may close.
