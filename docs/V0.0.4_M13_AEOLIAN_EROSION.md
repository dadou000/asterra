# Orbit V0.0.4 — M13 GPU Aeolian Erosion

Status: **implemented and verified**

Branch: `orbit`

Verified implementation head: `e869fba279646b0a4ccd100228524c2154e82702`

Implemented: 2026-09-18

M13 adds wind-driven erosion and deposition over the same M08 physical
material column used by hydraulic and thermal processes. It does not create a
separate wind heightfield.

## 1. Forcing contract

M13 consumes one coarse wind/process sample per physical cell:

```text
east wind velocity      m/s
north wind velocity     m/s
surface resistance      [0,1]
```

Wind components are expressed in the page-local M01 tangent frame. Positive
north is converted to the page-grid north direction internally.

`surfaceResistance` is deliberately generic at this milestone. It can represent
authored protection, vegetation drag or other process resistance without making
M13 own biome/vegetation assets before the later biome milestones exist.

## 2. Airborne state

M13 keeps two transient mobile lanes:

```text
airborne sand
airborne soil / fines
```

The CPU reference stores kilograms per physical cell.

The GPU stores kilograms per square meter in an `N² × float2` buffer:

```text
x = sand
y = soil/fines
```

These are process state, not terrain authority. Physical deposited material
always returns to M08 sand/soil layers.

M14 later generalizes the same distinction into the unified shared sediment
exchange contract for hydraulic, thermal and aeolian processes.

## 3. Wind direction and terrain exposure

Each local wind vector is quantized to one of eight page-local transport
directions.

Exposure is evaluated by tracing several cells upwind against the actual M08
surface:

```text
bedrock + regolith + soil + sand + debris
```

The solver tracks the maximum positive upwind obstruction slope.

A lee shadow uses:

```text
lee factor = exp(-shadowStrength × obstructionSlope)
```

while an exposed windward face may receive a bounded capacity boost.

This makes shelter a physical carrying-capacity effect rather than a cosmetic
wind mask.

## 4. Carrying capacity

Local carrying capacity is:

```text
capacity [kg/m²]
  = K
  × windSpeed ^ exponent
  × exposure
  × moisture suppression
  × surface-resistance allowance
```

Moisture suppression is derived directly from the M08 normalized moisture
channel:

```text
dryness = (1 - moisture) ^ moistureExponent
```

and resistance contributes:

```text
allowance = 1 - surfaceResistance
```

Wet or strongly resistant/vegetated surfaces therefore lose carrying/pickup
capacity without changing the supplied wind vector.

## 5. Pickup and deposition

If local airborne load is below capacity, M13 may pick up the currently exposed
mobile surface:

```text
sand → airborne sand
soil → airborne fines
```

Pickup is bounded by:

- carrying-capacity deficit;
- configured pickup rate;
- available exposed layer depth;
- maximum pickup depth per step.

If airborne load exceeds capacity, the excess deposits locally:

```text
airborne sand  → M08 sand
airborne fines → M08 soil
```

Lee shadows therefore naturally produce deposition because their local
capacity falls below the incoming transported load.

## 6. Reptation

A configurable fraction of freshly lifted sand remains in the near-surface
reptation lane rather than entering saltation.

Reptated mass is deposited one immediate D8 cell downwind.

This directly couples local grain creep/hopping to the physical M08 sand layer
and helps feed dune slip faces without introducing another terrain
representation.

## 7. Saltation transport

After pickup/deposition, the remaining airborne state is advected one local D8
wind cell per iteration.

The transported fraction is bounded by:

```text
saltationRate
× dt
× windSpeed / referenceWindSpeed
```

and clamped to `[0,1]`.

The CPU implementation uses deterministic ordered accumulation.

The GPU implementation uses a gather pass: each receiver inspects the eight
neighboring exchange proposals that target it. This avoids floating-point
scatter atomics.

M13 physical page boundaries are closed. Airborne mass that would leave the
page remains in the edge cell. M14 owns explicit cross-page sediment transport
flux.

## 8. Dune avalanching

Aeolian deposition is followed by an explicit **sand-only** avalanche stage.

An exposed sand cell finds its steepest lower D8 neighbor. If the slope exceeds
the configured sand repose angle, the source transfers enough sand toward that
neighbor to relax the excess slope, bounded by:

- available sand depth;
- avalanche relaxation;
- maximum avalanche depth per iteration.

This is not delegated to arbitrary height smoothing. Only exposed sand moves in
the M13 slip-face stage.

The default repose target is 33 degrees.

## 9. Bedrock abrasion

If all loose M08 layers are absent, exposed rock can contribute sediment
through abrasion.

Abrasion scales with:

- wind speed cubed relative to reference wind;
- M02 intrinsic aeolian erodibility;
- local exposure;
- moisture suppression;
- surface resistance.

The base abrasion rate is intentionally orders of magnitude smaller than loose
sand pickup.

Removed rock mass enters the airborne sand lane. Because M08 reference bedrock
height is unchanged, the physical mass ledger continues to report that rock as
excavated substrate until its mass is deposited back into loose material.

## 10. CPU material conservation

For closed M13 pages:

```text
initial loose mass
+ newly abraded bedrock mass
=
final loose mass
+ final airborne sand/fines mass
```

Reptation and dune avalanching are internal transfers and therefore disappear
from the global balance.

The CPU acceptance suite checks the independent M08 physical-column balance,
not only process counters.

## 11. GPU representation

`GpuAeolianErosionPage` owns persistent fixed-capacity resources:

```text
airborne A/B          N² × float2   (8 B/cell)
exchange proposal     N² × 40 B
avalanche proposal    N² × 8 B
M02 geology table     32 B/material
material snapshot     N² × 24 B
```

No aeolian GPU resource allocation occurs per simulation step.

Wind forcing is supplied as one `float4` per cell:

```text
x = east wind m/s
y = north wind m/s
z = surface resistance [0,1]
w = reserved
```

## 12. GPU pass order

The GPU implementation uses seven kernels:

1. initialize airborne state;
2. compute exposure/capacity and local exchange proposal;
3. apply pickup/deposition and gather reptation;
4. compute sand-avalanche proposals;
5. gather/apply sand avalanche;
6. gather saltation into the next airborne buffer;
7. snapshot physical material for CPU-authority readback.

The physical passes operate directly on the M08 GPU texture lanes.

## 13. CPU authority handoff

M08 remains CPU terrain authority.

After GPU simulation, `RecordMaterialReadback()` snapshots:

```text
bedrock
regolith
soil
sand
debris
moisture
```

into the standard 24-byte-per-cell buffer representation.

After the submission fence completes,
`ApplyMaterialReadbackToCpu()` updates the matching
`MaterialColumnPage` while preserving stable rock identity and reference
bedrock accounting.

## 14. Acceptance coverage

`Orbit.TerrainAeolianErosion` verifies:

- an upwind obstacle creates a measurable lee shadow;
- transported available sand accumulates into a lee-side depositional dune;
- the closed-page M08/airborne material balance remains conserved;
- high moisture strongly suppresses sand pickup;
- high surface/vegetation resistance strongly suppresses pickup;
- exposed rock abrasion occurs but is much slower than loose sand transport;
- exposed soil/fines enter the airborne fine-sediment lane;
- fixed inputs produce deterministic process and material state.

`Orbit.TerrainGpuAeolianShaders` DXC-compiles all seven M13 kernels.

## 16. Next boundary

M14 — Unified Sediment Exchange should replace the process-local mobile lanes
with one shared typed sediment state covering at least:

- sand;
- soil/fines;
- coarse debris;
- suspended/mobile water sediment where applicable;
- process conversion/deposition rules;
- explicit cross-page transport boundary flux.

Hydraulic deposition must become available to wind after drying, wind must be
able to expose rock covered by previous deposits, and thermal collapse debris
must be consumable by later processes without creating parallel terrain
heightfields.
