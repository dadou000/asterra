## M30-01 — Stratigraphy exposure

The validation starts from the authored M03 `continental_shelf.orbitstratigraphy` reference profile and a real M08 `MaterialColumnPage`.

The physical bedrock surface begins 0.5 m below the virtual stratigraphic top in volcanic ash. M08 bedrock erosion then lowers that same physical surface through two conceptual layer boundaries. M03 is sampled at the resulting M08 bedrock height and must expose, in order:

```text
volcanic ash
    ↓ 2 m physical incision
sandstone
    ↓ 6 m further incision
limestone
```

The test also replays the first incision on a second identical material page and requires identical bedrock height, removed mass and resolved stratum identity. This freezes the handoff between mutable M08 physical depth and M03's virtual layer stack without voxelizing the strata or introducing render/clipmap state.

## M30-02 — Bedrock stripping

Case 2 freezes the loose-cover → exposed-bedrock transition in the canonical M08 material column.

The fixture places an exact 1.0 m loose stack over authored volcanic-ash bedrock using binary-exact layer depths:

```text
0.125 m debris
0.125 m sand
0.250 m soil
0.500 m regolith
----------------
1.000 m loose cover
bedrock
```

Validation proceeds in three stages:

1. 0.75 m erosion must remove debris, sand, soil and only the upper 0.25 m of regolith. Bedrock height must remain unchanged and regolith must remain the exposed surface.
2. The final 0.25 m must remove the remaining regolith exactly, expose bedrock, preserve both current/reference bedrock heights and report zero excavated-bedrock mass.
3. Only a subsequent 0.5 m erosion request may lower the M08 physical bedrock surface. That incision must enter mass accounting using the exposed M02 rock density.

The loose-material removal ledger is checked against the page's authoritative per-material densities and cell area. This prevents three regressions at once: erosion skipping the fixed loose stack, exact-cover removal accidentally shaving bedrock, and loose-material stripping being misreported as geological excavation.

## M30-03 — Sediment deposition/burial

Case 3 freezes the canonical M14 mobile-sediment → M08 physical-column burial path required by the final integration gate.

The test begins with genuinely exposed authored bedrock and publishes one typed waterborne M14 load chosen to resolve into binary-exact M08 physical depths:

```text
coarse debris -> 0.125 m M08 debris
sand          -> 0.250 m M08 sand
fines         -> 0.500 m M08 soil
-----------------------------------
total burial     0.875 m
```

`SedimentExchangePage::DepositToColumn()` must transfer the complete load into M08 exactly once, empty the selected mobile inventory, and record the transfer as `mobileToPhysical`. The resulting loose-material mass must equal the original M14 payload.

Burial is not geological mutation: current/reference bedrock heights, M02 rock identity and the sampled M03 virtual stratum must remain unchanged while the physical M08 surface rises by the deposited loose depth. Stripping exactly that 0.875 m cover must then re-expose the same bedrock with zero bedrock incision and the same stratigraphic identity.

This case prevents renderer-only burial, duplicated sediment authority, lost mobile mass and deposition that accidentally rewrites geological substrate.

## M30-04 — Hydraulic mass conservation

Case 4 promotes the stable closed-page M11 hydraulic slope reference into the cross-system M30 gate and checks the complete M08/M11/M14 sediment ledger, not only the solver-local totals.

The deterministic 7×7 physical page contains a 0.8 m/cell downslope bedrock gradient with 0.20 m regolith + 0.20 m soil cover. Uniform rainfall plus an uphill source strip drives measurable erosion, downstream deposition and a non-zero suspended remainder while all physical page boundaries remain closed.

The required equalities are:

```text
M11 eroded mass
    = M11 deposited mass
    + final suspended mass

M14 physicalToMobile
    = M11 eroded mass

M14 mobileToPhysical
    = M11 deposited mass

M14 final waterborne inventory
    = M11 final suspended mass
```

Per-cell M11 typed sediment and the legacy scalar compatibility channel must both match the same M14 waterborne inventory. M14 import/export accounting and M11 boundary loss must remain zero because this case is intentionally closed-page.

Finally, an independent M08 check recomputes `initial loose + excavated bedrock - final loose - suspended`. It must match M11's physical-column error and remain within the established f32 physical-layer tolerance. This catches hidden sediment stores, double counting, scalar-only mass, and erosion/deposition that bypasses the shared M14 exchange.

## M30-05 — Aeolian mass conservation

Case 5 promotes the established M13 lee-dune reference into the cross-system M30 gate. A 9×9 physical page carries 0.25 m of sand over basalt, with a 2.5 m obstacle strip and deterministic 14 m/s east wind. The solve must exercise sand pickup, lee-side deposition, saltation, reptation and explicit east-edge export.

The physical conservation identity is:

```text
initial M08 loose mass
+ newly abraded M08 bedrock
= final M08 loose mass
+ final M14 mobile mass
+ M14 boundary export
```

The corresponding M14 exchange ledger must independently close as:

```text
physicalToMobile
= mobileToPhysical
+ exported
+ remaining mobile
```

M13's reported `finalAirborneMassKg` and `boundaryLossKg` must equal the canonical M14 inventory/export accounting. Per-cell M13 airborne diagnostics must sum to that same final mobile inventory, the aeolian-only solve must not create waterborne sediment, and reptation must visibly use M14's surface-mobile transport lane.

An independent M08 mass query verifies final loose material and newly excavated bedrock against the M13 balance. The fixture also retains its lee-shadow/deposition assertions so conservation is tested across real erosion/transport/deposition behavior rather than a no-op wind field.

## M30-06 — Thermal repose convergence

Case 6 promotes the M12 local thermal-relaxation reference into the M30 gate with an explicit geometric convergence check.

A 5×5 M08 page carries 2.0 m of exposed sand over basalt. The center bedrock cell is raised by 3.0 m, producing an initial sand-covered peak more than 20° above the configured 33° sand angle of repose. Bedrock fracture is disabled so the only legal response is redistribution of the existing loose sand.

The acceptance requires M12 to:

```text
perform non-zero internal loose-material transfer
converge within the bounded iteration budget
finish with exposed-sand slopes <= repose + convergence tolerance
preserve moved-out mass == received mass
preserve total M08 loose mass
leave bedrock height/reference/material identity unchanged
```

The resulting converged physical page is then passed through `SimulateThermalErosion()` a second time with identical inputs. That replay must converge in one iteration and leave every authoritative M08 physical layer bit-for-bit unchanged. This freezes thermal stability as an actual fixed point rather than merely a solver flag.

No M14 mobile authority participates in this case: M12 talus/loose redistribution remains physical M08 state until a later transport process explicitly picks it up through M14.

## M30-07 — Cross-page water flux

Case 7 freezes the M25 physical boundary-transfer contract for conservative water exchange across both a cube-face edge and a diagonal corner.

A level-2 physical page on the +X cube face exports four uniquely sized water packets through its east edge plus one north-east corner packet. The east edge crosses onto another cube face, so this fixture exercises the canonical M01 neighbor mapping, sample-order reversal where required, and receiver-local momentum rotation rather than only same-face copying.

The acceptance requires:

```text
sum(source edge + corner water)
    = SurfaceBoundaryTransferBatch total

receiver edge incoming volume
    = exact source east-edge volume

receiver diagonal incoming volume
    = exact source corner volume

source outward momentum
    -> receiver inward local momentum
```

Each edge sample is tagged by a unique volume and tangent component. After remapping, the target sample index must match `RemapTileEdgeSampleIndex()` and the conservative `velocityMoment` must equal `TransformBoundaryVectorAcrossEdge()` exactly within floating-point tolerance.

The same two physical page fluxes are submitted in forward and reversed scheduler order. Edge/corner transfer ordering, targets, revisions, sample packets and total water volume must be identical. This prevents scheduler order, render clipmaps, camera state or cache residency from becoming part of physical cross-page water identity.

## M30-08 — Cross-page sediment flux

Case 8 freezes the canonical M14 → M25 → M14 physical-page sediment exchange across a real cube-face boundary without involving a process-specific erosion solver.

Three source edge samples carry deliberately different typed payloads and transport media:

```text
sample 0: waterborne     = 1 kg sand + 2 kg fines + 3 kg coarse
sample 1: airborne      = 4 kg sand + 5 kg fines + 6 kg coarse
sample 2: surfaceMobile = 7 kg sand + 8 kg fines + 9 kg coarse
```

Each payload is exported through the +X page's east edge using the production `SedimentExchangePage::ExportAcrossBoundary()` path. `TakeOutgoingBoundaryFlux()` then hands the canonical M14 packet to M25, which remaps it across the cube face before `ApplySedimentBoundaryTransfers()` imports it into the receiver M14 page.

The acceptance requires class-by-class and medium-by-medium conservation. Sample order must follow `RemapTileEdgeSampleIndex()`, and transport diagnostics must rotate through `TransformBoundaryVectorAcrossEdge()` into receiver-local east/north orientation. No payload may leak into another medium.

Source export accounting must equal receiver import accounting exactly, the source mobile inventory must be empty after full export, and the combined two-page mobile mass must remain equal to the original payload. This prevents cross-page loss, duplication, class mixing, medium mixing, and cube-face orientation errors before higher-level dune or hydraulic migration tests are considered.

## M30-09 — Cross-page dune migration

Case 9 is the process-driven multi-page aeolian acceptance. Unlike case 8, the boundary payload is not injected synthetically: M13 must create it by eroding physical M08 sand on the source page.

The source page contains a two-cell-wide sand sheet along its downwind edge and is solved under deterministic 14 m/s east wind. Bedrock abrasion and soil pickup are disabled so any exported material is unambiguously dune sand. The source M13 solve must produce measurable M14 boundary export and its reported boundary loss must match the M14 export ledger.

The resulting M14 boundary flux is handed to M25 and imported into the adjacent physical page. The receiver then runs M13 with that imported M14 page as its initial mobile state. This required a permanent M13 continuation contract: `SimulateAeolianErosion()` now accepts an optional compatible `SedimentExchangePage`, preserves its accounting history, includes its mobile inventory in the initial mass ledger, and reports only newly generated boundary export for the current solve.

On the calm receiver page the imported mobile sand must deposit into authoritative M08 sand, leaving no remaining mobile mass and no new receiver boundary loss. The imported M14 accounting ledger must survive the M13 solve intact.

The final two-page acceptance is:

```text
initial source physical sand
+ initial receiver physical sand
=
final source physical sand
+ final source mobile sand
+ final receiver physical sand
+ final receiver mobile sand
```

within the established f32 material-column tolerance. This freezes actual dune migration across physical page ownership rather than merely verifying packet transport.

## M30-10 — FastFlow drainage reference

Case 10 freezes the mathematical reference that the M09 GPU rake-compress/FastFlow accumulation must reproduce.

The fixture is a 7×7 M08 physical page with non-uniform runoff, a deterministic east-draining catchment, and one artificial interior pit. `FillToBoundary` must condition that pit without changing the raw M08 surface, so the validation exercises the actual M09 conditioned routing surface rather than a trivial monotonic heightfield.

After production `BuildDrainagePage()` creates the D8 graph, the test builds an independent inverse-donor graph from the resulting downstream links. A leaf-driven topological reduction computes reference contributing area and discharge without using M09's descending-elevation accumulation implementation.

For every physical cell:

```text
M09 drainageArea == independent donor-subtree area
M09 discharge    == independent donor-subtree runoff discharge
```

Every internal edge must be strictly downhill on `drainageElevationMeters`, the donor graph must contain no cycles, and all conditioned cells must have a downstream route. Summing drainage area and discharge over every page-exit cell must recover the entire catchment area and the exact sum of all local runoff inputs.

The fixture also requires meaningful multi-donor accumulation and repeats the full M09 build with identical inputs, requiring bit-identical conditioned elevations, flow targets, area, discharge and revision identity. This is the CPU correctness reference for the production GPU FastFlow rake-compress path; GPU execution remains covered separately by Orbit's drainage shader/workstation tests.

## M30-11 — Authored canyon persistence

Case 11 promotes M04 authored canyon intent into a full cross-system terrain-process acceptance rather than re-testing the M04 serializer.

The fixture starts from a regenerated upstream M08 surface whose center row is deliberately a shallow ridge. A canonical M04 spline crossing the physical page authors three independent fields on that same centerline:

```text
height offset  = -24 m
protection     = 0.60
drainage       = +1.0
```

M05 samples that project authority into `MacroGeologyField`; `BuildStreamPowerForcing()` forwards the authored height/protection values into M10, while M09 receives drainage guidance sampled from the same M05 field. An otherwise identical un-authored macro field provides the control solve.

The acceptance requires the authored centerline to remain substantially lower than the control after iterative M09/M10 regeneration and to capture more contributing drainage area than the control centerline. This proves the canyon affects terrain-process behavior rather than existing only as a cosmetic offset.

The canyon remains physically erodible: M10 incision must be non-zero, while the 0.60 M04 protection field reduces erosion allowance rather than freezing the surface. `ApplyStreamPowerErosionResult()` must bake the solved canyon through the normal M10→M08 physical path.

Finally, all derived M05/M09/M10 state is rebuilt from the unchanged upstream M08 page and unchanged M04 constraint authority using a fresh `MacroGeologyField`. The regenerated solve must reproduce the same revision identity, physical result, incision, drainage area/discharge and flow graph bit-for-bit. This freezes authored canyon persistence as regeneration from project authority, not persistence of generated height pages.
